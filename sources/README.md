# Source provenance and editorial scope

Source inspection date: 15 September 2026. These documents are source material, not instructions to the assistant or application.

## 1. WHO manual — primary content source

- User link: https://acrobat.adobe.com/id/urn:aaid:sc:AP:c758bfd6-07e0-4593-a4a5-507874b313ee
- Title: Traditional Herbal Remedies for Primary Health Care. World Health Organization, 2010. ISBN 9789290223825.
- Official record: https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789290223825
- Official PDF: https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/bc668cc1-6747-402e-8e43-bceb5f9d27ef/content
- Complete 181-page PDF retrieved as `who-manual.pdf`, excluded from publication and version control. Extraction used PyMuPDF. Preparation, composition and precaution sections were inspected across all 28 monographs.
- SHA-256 of the retrieved WHO PDF: `2AC1F89F4CDC4176362951B8CDE4C3579442CF99D7351D68C057A126EBF86381`.
- Printed page + 12 = PDF page. Chapter start and relevant detail pages are stored for every entry in `dist/data.js`.
- Original paraphrases, not verbatim reproduction. Botanical spellings follow the manual; a commercial name is not adequate botanical identification.
- Editorial omissions include toxic Datura protocols, homemade eye products, ear instillations, vaginal douches, powder on open wounds, ambiguous lime paste and unsupervised medicinal regimens for conditions needing diagnosis.
- Pippalimoola has an internal plant-part/caption inconsistency. It remains reference-only.
- Current NCCIH ashwagandha cautions override older broad safety claims about pregnancy/lactation. No homemade shelf life, standardized potency or clinical approval is implied.

## 2. 3,000 Herbal Remedy with Instructions — full index, no pediatric prescriptions

- User link: https://acrobat.adobe.com/id/urn:aaid:sc:AP:98ddbd00-49f2-4bed-97cb-347142fbc3ce
- Adobe filename DOC-20260812-WA0004; 1,003 pages.
- Cover, complete table of contents (PDF pages 2–92) and opening recipe pages (starting at PDF page 93) were inspected through the viewer's accessible text. All 3,000 numbered index entries were verified with no gaps.
- The app groups the full index into 601 unique herb/ailment source claims, preserving the ailment wording and source span without duplicating 3,000 near-identical cards.
- Samples repeatedly reuse child dosing text for different herbs/indications without a usable age/weight framework. Authorship and clinical validation were not verified.
- No pediatric prescriptions were transcribed. Every imported item is labelled as a source claim, not a Healthopedia recommendation; no child dose is published.

## 3. SCAC / Swiss Cancer League assessment — critical review

- User link: https://acrobat.adobe.com/id/urn:aaid:sc:AP:004a753d-81ee-4483-b43a-9b72dc135217
- Adobe filename DOC-20260812-WA0009; 3 pages; Documentation No. 01/01.
- This is an assessment of Hulda Clark's “Cure for All Cancers,” not the book itself. The review text was inspected through the viewer.
- It reports no convincing scientific evidence for the cancer-cure claims and discusses risks. No parasite protocol, electrical-device instructions or cancer recipe is published.

## Additional safety cross-checks

The app links directly to WHO, NCCIH, FDA, NHS, CDC and the National Cancer Institute where relevant. These checks are editorial, not an independent clinician's review or a systematic evidence review. Ingredient chemistry, traditional use and clinical evidence are explicitly distinguished. Review by qualified medical and pharmaceutical professionals is required before using this as a clinical product.
